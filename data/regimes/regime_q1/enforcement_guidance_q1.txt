Regulation A — Enforcement Guidance Bulletin Q1-2024-EG-001 (Effective Q1 2024)

This bulletin provides authoritative interpretation of Section 4.1 breach notification timelines.

Q: Does the 72-hour clock begin at the moment of initial suspicion or confirmed discovery?
A: The 72-hour period begins when the data controller has sufficient information to conclude
   that a personal data breach has occurred. Preliminary investigation to confirm suspicion
   does not pause the clock if the investigation itself takes more than 24 hours.

Q: Are third-party processors' discovery events attributable to the data controller?
A: Yes. If a data processor discovers a breach affecting the data controller's personal data,
   the 72-hour clock begins when the processor notifies the controller, provided that processor
   notification occurs within 12 hours of the processor's own discovery.

Q: What constitutes "high risk" for purposes of individual notification under Section 4.3?
A: High risk is presumed where the compromised data includes financial account credentials,
   medical information, or government-issued identification numbers. Controllers may rebut
   this presumption by demonstrating that the data was rendered unintelligible (e.g., encrypted)
   prior to or at the time of the breach and the encryption key was not compromised.

Penalty Safe Harbor:
A data controller that has implemented a documented incident response plan reviewed within
the prior 12 months and makes a good-faith notification effort within 72 hours, even if
incomplete, will not be subject to the enhanced "repeated violation" surcharge under Section 5.3
for a first occurrence of late filing.
