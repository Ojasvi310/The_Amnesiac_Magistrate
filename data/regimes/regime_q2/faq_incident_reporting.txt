Regulation B — Frequently Asked Questions: AI Incident Reporting (Effective Q2 2024)

Q1: Does Section 4.1's 48-hour clock apply to incidents discovered by a third-party vendor
    running the AI system on a deployer's behalf?
A1: Yes. The deployer bears primary reporting responsibility. Vendor contracts must include
    a clause requiring the vendor to notify the deployer within 12 hours of discovering a
    material-harm incident. The 48-hour clock for the deployer's regulatory report begins
    upon deployer notification by the vendor.

Q2: Our AI system flagged a loan applicant incorrectly. The error was caught internally and
    corrected before any formal decision was issued. Is this a reportable incident?
A2: No, provided the correction occurred before the output was used to make or substantially
    influence a final decision affecting the individual. Internal quality-control catches that
    are remediated prior to output issuance are not reportable under Section 4.1. Deployers
    should nonetheless log such events internally for Conformity Assessment update purposes.

Q3: What constitutes a "material change" triggering a Conformity Assessment update under 2.2?
A3: Material changes include: retraining on substantially different data (>30% new data by
    volume), a change in the model architecture or inference pipeline, expansion to a new
    decision domain not covered in the original assessment, or any change that alters accuracy
    or demographic parity metrics by more than 5 percentage points on the original test set.

Q4: Can a Conformity Assessment prepared under Regulation A's data governance requirements
    satisfy Regulation B's Section 2.1 requirements?
A4: Only partially. A Regulation A-compliant data governance section may be incorporated by
    reference, but Regulation B additionally requires bias disaggregation metrics and a
    foreseeable-misuse analysis that Regulation A does not mandate. A combined assessment
    document covering both regulations is permissible and encouraged.
